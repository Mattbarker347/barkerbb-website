Group benefits help for Andover employers

The question Andover owners ask us first is almost never which plan. It is how much of it they are expected to pay.

There is no single right answer, and anybody who gives you one without asking about your margins is guessing. What there is instead is a set of structures, each with a different effect on your budget, on how many employees take the plan, and on how the cost moves as your team gets older.

Carriers do require a minimum employer contribution for a group plan to issue, and the minimum varies by carrier, so that sets a floor. Above the floor it is a business decision, and it is worth making it deliberately once rather than inheriting whatever the last broker set up.

Ways employers split the cost

A share of the employee-only rate

The most common structure. Predictable for you, and it treats everybody the same regardless of who they cover.

A flat amount per paycheck

Easy for employees to understand and easy for you to budget, because it does not move when rates do.

Tiered by who is covered

Contributing more where an employee covers a family, which is where affordability usually breaks down.

Generous on the basics

Covering dental, vision or life in full while employees share more of the medical. Often the best value per dollar.

Why your split affects your rates

Contribution and participation are connected. Carriers require a minimum share of eligible employees to enroll, and the fastest way to fall short is a split that makes the plan unaffordable for the people you most want covered. So the contribution question is not only a budget question, it decides whether the plan issues at all. We model it both ways before you commit to anything.

We quote across Blue Cross Blue Shield of Minnesota, HealthPartners, Medica, UnitedHealthcare, and the regional carriers, and we round the package out with dental, vision, life and disability, and supplemental coverage. One conversation is usually enough to tell whether we can help.
