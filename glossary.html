Benefits Glossary

Benefits language, in plain English.

Benefits are full of words that get used as though everybody already knows them. Here are 42 of them, written the way we would explain them to you on the phone. If something here still does not make sense, call us and ask.

A C D E F G H I L M N O P Q S V W

A

A

Accident insurance

A supplemental policy that pays cash directly to an employee after a covered accident, on top of whatever their medical plan pays.
A

Age-banded rates

A pricing method where each employee has their own premium based on their age, rather than everyone paying the same rate.

C

C

Cafeteria plan (Section 125 plan)

A written plan document that lets employees pay their share of benefit premiums with pre-tax dollars.
C

Census

The employee roster a carrier needs in order to quote your group, usually date of birth, zip code, and who is enrolling.
C

COBRA

A federal right that lets employees keep their group health coverage for a limited time after they would otherwise lose it.
C

Coinsurance

The share of a medical bill an employee pays as a percentage, after the deductible has been met.
C

Composite rates

A pricing method where every employee in the same coverage tier pays the same premium regardless of age.
C

Copay

A flat dollar amount an employee pays for a specific service, like an office visit or a prescription.
C

Creditable coverage

Prescription coverage that is at least as good as the Medicare Part D standard, which employers must disclose to employees each year.
C

Critical illness insurance

A supplemental policy that pays a lump sum of cash when an employee is diagnosed with a covered serious illness.

D

D

Deductible

The amount an employee pays for covered care each plan year before the medical plan starts paying its share.

E

E

Employer contribution

The share of the premium the employer pays, which drives both the employee payroll deduction and whether the carrier will issue the plan.
E

Explanation of benefits (EOB)

The statement a carrier sends after processing a claim, showing what was billed, what the plan paid, and what the member owes.

F

F

Flexible spending account (FSA)

An employer-established account that lets employees set aside pre-tax pay for eligible medical or dependent care expenses.
F

Formulary

The list of prescription drugs a plan covers, organized into tiers that determine what the member pays.
F

Fully insured

The traditional funding model where the employer pays a fixed premium and the carrier takes all the claims risk.

G

G

Guaranteed issue

Coverage a carrier must offer without medical underwriting, so no one can be turned down or surcharged for their health.

H

H

Health reimbursement arrangement (HRA)

An employer-funded account that reimburses employees for eligible medical costs, with the employer keeping anything unspent.
H

Health savings account (HSA)

An employee-owned savings account for medical expenses, available only to people enrolled in a qualifying high deductible plan.
H

High deductible health plan (HDHP)

A plan with a deductible at or above an IRS threshold, which is what makes an employee eligible to contribute to an HSA.
H

HMO

A plan design built around a defined network, usually with no out-of-network coverage except emergencies.
H

Hospital indemnity insurance

A supplemental policy that pays a set cash amount for a hospital admission and for each day of a covered stay.

I

I

ICHRA (CHOICE Arrangement)

An arrangement where the employer gives employees a tax-free allowance to buy their own individual health coverage instead of offering a group plan.

L

L

Level funded

A funding model where the employer pays a steady monthly amount like a premium, but gets money back if claims come in low.
L

Long term disability (LTD)

Coverage that replaces part of an employee’s income when a disability keeps them out of work beyond the short term.

M

M

Minimum participation

The share of eligible employees a carrier requires to enroll before it will issue or renew a group plan.
M

Minnesota Paid Leave

Minnesota’s state paid family and medical leave program, which employers fund through the state or through an approved private plan.

N

N

Network

The group of doctors, clinics and hospitals that have agreed to contracted rates with a carrier.

O

O

Open enrollment

The window each plan year when employees can join, drop or change coverage without needing a qualifying event.
O

Out-of-pocket maximum

The most an employee can pay for covered in-network care in a plan year, after which the plan pays the rest in full.

P

P

PPO

A plan design that covers care both inside and outside its network, paying more when the member stays in network.
P

Premium

The fixed amount paid each month to keep coverage in force, split between the employer and the employee.

Q

Q

Qualifying life event

A change in circumstances, like marriage or losing other coverage, that lets an employee change their benefits outside open enrollment.

S

S

Self funded

A funding model where the employer pays employee claims directly out of its own money instead of paying a carrier a premium.
S

Short term disability (STD)

Coverage that replaces part of an employee’s income for a limited period when illness or injury keeps them out of work.
S

Special enrollment

A limited window outside open enrollment when an employee may change coverage because of a qualifying life event.
S

Stop-loss insurance

Insurance that protects a self-funded or level-funded employer by capping how much it can pay in claims.
S

Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)

A short standardized document that describes what a plan covers and costs, in a format required to be the same across all carriers.
S

Summary Plan Description (SPD)

The plain-language document required under federal law that tells employees what the plan covers and what their rights are.

V

V

Voluntary benefits

Coverage offered through the employer but paid for by the employee, usually through payroll deduction.

W

W

Waiting period

The time a new hire must be employed before their coverage can begin.
W

Waiver of coverage

A signed record that an eligible employee declined the plan, and why, which protects the group’s participation math.
