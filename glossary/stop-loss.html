Benefits Glossary

Stop-loss insurance

Insurance that protects a self-funded or level-funded employer by capping how much it can pay in claims.

Stop-loss is what makes self funding survivable for a small employer. It's coverage the employer buys against its own claims, and it comes in two forms that do different jobs.

Specific and aggregate

Specific stop-loss caps what the employer pays on any one person, so a single catastrophic claim doesn't sink the plan. Aggregate stop-loss caps what the employer pays across everyone combined, protecting against a year where lots of moderate claims add up. A plan usually carries both.

Read the contract basis

How the policy defines which claims it covers, by when they're incurred and when they're paid, determines whether a claim at a year boundary is covered or lands in a gap. It's technical and it's exactly where an unpleasant surprise comes from.

Quick Facts

  • ProtectsThe employer, not the member
  • SpecificCaps cost per person
  • AggregateCaps total cost

Related terms

S

Self funded

A funding model where the employer pays employee claims directly out of its own money instead of paying a carrier a premium.

L

Level funded

A funding model where the employer pays a steady monthly amount like a premium, but gets money back if claims come in low.

F

Fully insured

The traditional funding model where the employer pays a fixed premium and the carrier takes all the claims risk.
