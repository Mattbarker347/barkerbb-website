Benefits Glossary

Out-of-pocket maximum

The most an employee can pay for covered in-network care in a plan year, after which the plan pays the rest in full.

The out-of-pocket maximum is the ceiling. Once a member's deductible, coinsurance and qualifying copays add up to that figure, the plan pays covered in-network care in full for the rest of the year.

The number worth communicating

Employees fixate on the deductible, but the out-of-pocket maximum is the one that answers the question they're really asking, which is what the worst year looks like. Lead with it when you explain a plan.

What it does not cap

Premiums don't count toward it. Out-of-network care often has a separate and higher limit, or none at all. And charges for services the plan doesn't cover don't count either. The ceiling protects against covered in-network care, which is not the same as protecting against all cost.

Quick Facts

  • CapsCovered in-network member cost
  • ExcludesPremiums
  • Out of networkOften a separate, higher limit
  • ResetsEach plan year

Related terms

D

Deductible

The amount an employee pays for covered care each plan year before the medical plan starts paying its share.

C

Coinsurance

The share of a medical bill an employee pays as a percentage, after the deductible has been met.

C

Copay

A flat dollar amount an employee pays for a specific service, like an office visit or a prescription.

N

Network

The group of doctors, clinics and hospitals that have agreed to contracted rates with a carrier.
