We are a few minutes up the road in Ramsey, so Anoka is our own back yard. Employers here run the range from downtown storefronts along the Rum River to service and contracting companies working across the north metro.

Group benefits help for Anoka employers

Most Anoka employers call us in the same month: the one the renewal shows up in.

A renewal arriving with an increase feels like a bill you have to pay. It is actually a decision with four separate levers, and most employers only ever get told about one of them. Accepting the number as offered, or switching carriers in a panic, are not the only two options on the table.

What we do first is separate what moved. Age of the group, plan design, carrier trend and your own contribution all push the number, and they are not fixed together. Once you can see which one did the damage, the decision usually gets simpler rather than harder.

The four levers you actually have

Plan design

Deductible, out of pocket maximum and network. Changing the design changes the premium without changing carriers.

Carrier

Worth shopping every year, because the carriers do not all move in the same direction at the same time.

Your contribution

How much of the cost the business covers, and whether the split is the same for every tier.

How the plan is funded

Fully insured is not the only option once a group reaches a certain size and stability.

Timing, and why waiting costs you options

Minnesota carriers reprice on a quarterly cycle, so a quote has a shelf life of roughly one cycle, and shopping late in your renewal window narrows what is available rather than widening it. If your plan renews on January 1, which most do, the useful conversation happens in the fall and not in December. Come to us early and the worst case is that we tell you to keep what you have.

We quote across Blue Cross Blue Shield of Minnesota, HealthPartners, Medica, UnitedHealthcare, and the regional carriers, and we round the package out with dental, vision, life and disability, and supplemental coverage. One conversation is usually enough to tell whether we can help.
