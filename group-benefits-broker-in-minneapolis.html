The hardest benefits problem in a city market is usually not cost, it is participation. A younger team often looks at the payroll deduction, decides they are healthy, and declines. That feels like a saving until the carrier counts your enrollment and your rates move the wrong way.

When your team is young and keeps declining coverage

Why they decline

Younger employees weigh a monthly deduction against a benefit they do not expect to use. Cost is the objection, but perceived value is the real one.

HSA-eligible plans

A lower premium paired with a health savings account gives a young employee something that feels like their money, because it is.

Voluntary lines carry weight

Accident and hospital coverage cost a few dollars a paycheck and pay cash. For a twenty-six-year-old that is far more tangible than a deductible.

Participation drives your rate

Carriers set minimum enrollment. Falling under it can cost you the plan entirely, so participation is a business problem, not an HR one.

Who we work with

Minneapolis employers from a couple of employees up to around two hundred, whether you're setting up group benefits for the first time or you've had a plan for years and your renewal just came back with an increase that made you wince. Both are good reasons to get a second set of eyes on it.

Explaining benefits to people who have never had them

A lot of city employers hire people for whom this is a first real benefits package, and nobody has ever walked them through what a deductible is or why the cheapest plan is not always the cheapest. We run that meeting in plain language, and we take the questions afterward directly so your managers do not have to become the benefits help desk.

However we get there, the process is the same five steps for every group we take on. You can read it on our locations page.
